# User guide

Written for everyday use, not for a developer. Nothing here needs any technical
knowledge.

---

## The five tabs

Along the bottom of the screen:

| Tab | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Home** | The dashboard: what you have, what came in, what went out, what is still to pay |
| **Money** | Every transaction, searchable and filterable |
| **Savings** | Your savings goals and how they are getting on |
| **Reports** | Printable summaries and charts |
| **More** | Accounts, people, regular payments, categories, import, settings |

---

## Setting up, in the right order

Doing this in order takes about ten minutes and means everything afterwards
works by itself.

### 1. Add the people

**More → People → +**

Add yourself, your partner, and any children whose money you want to keep track
of. There is already a "Joint" person for shared money.

Give each person a colour — it is used on charts and lists to tell your figures
apart at a glance.

### 2. Add the accounts

**More → Accounts → +**

Add every place your money sits: current accounts, savings, cash, the coin jar,
credit cards, loans, the mortgage.

For each one:

- **Starting balance** — what is in it right now. Everything else is worked out
  from this plus the transactions you record, so if this is right, your balances
  stay right.
- **Balance as at** — today, unless you are entering historic transactions.
- **Warn me below** — the app will tell you when the balance drops under this.
- **Count towards net worth** — leave on unless it is something you do not
  consider yours.

Credit cards, loans and mortgages are money you owe. Enter their balance as a
**negative** number, and the app will show it as a debt and subtract it from
your net worth.

### 3. Add the regular payments

**More → Regular payments → +**

This is the step that removes most of the work. Set up each thing that repeats:

- Salary and any other regular income
- The mortgage or rent, council tax, energy, water, broadband, mobile
- Insurance, car finance, road tax, subscriptions
- The amount you put into savings each month

For each one, choose how often it repeats, when it is next due, which account it
comes from and who is responsible.

Two options are worth understanding:

- **"The amount changes each time"** — turn this on for energy, fuel and food.
  The app still posts the payment on time, but marks it "Check" so you can
  correct the figure when the real bill arrives.
- **"When it falls due"** — *Post automatically* is the default and is what you
  want for a fixed direct debit. *Ask me to confirm* posts it but leaves it
  flagged. *Remind me only* raises a notification without recording anything.

From then on, the app records these for you. You do not have to open it on the
1st of the month; the next time you do, everything you missed is already there.

### 4. Add your savings goals

**Savings → +**

Give each goal a name, a target, how much you are putting by each month, and
when you would like it by.

A goal can either be **linked to an account** — in which case that account's
balance is the goal's balance — or **tracked by hand**, where you add to it
yourself. Use a linked account when the money really does sit somewhere
separate, and a hand-tracked goal when you are saving inside a general account.

Each goal tells you not just how far along it is, but whether what you are
putting by will actually get you there in time.

## Day to day

### Recording something

**Money → Add**, or the **Add** button on the Home screen's "Recent" card.

- **Expense** — money leaving.
- **Income** — money arriving.
- **Transfer** — money moving between two of your own accounts. This is not
  spending, and the app does not count it as such. Use it for money you put into
  savings, and for paying a credit card off.

Only the description, amount, date and account are needed. Everything else is
optional.

### Confirming a variable bill

When a bill whose amount varies falls due, it appears with a **Check** tag. Tap
it, correct the amount, and save. Until you do, your figures are based on last
month's amount, which is why the tag is there.

### Finding something

The search box at the top of **Money** looks at names, notes, tags, categories,
accounts and people — so "hannah fuel" or "birthday" will find it, whichever
field you typed it in.

For anything more specific, tap the filter icon: date range, amount range, type,
category, person, account, and whether to include archived entries. The number
on the filter icon tells you how many filters are active.

### Correcting a mistake

Everything can be edited. Tap any entry to open it.

The three-dot menu on any row offers:

- **Duplicate** — makes a copy on today's date. Handy for an irregular payment
  you make often.
- **Archive** — hides it from lists but keeps it in your history and your
  totals.
- **Delete** — removes it permanently. Your totals change.

When in doubt, archive. It is the only one you can undo.

## Making sense of the numbers

### "Left to spend"

This is the figure a spreadsheet struggles with. It is:

> what has come in this month, less what has gone out, less the bills that are
> still to come before the month ends

So it is not "what is in my account" — it is what you can actually spend without
missing a bill.

### Balances

An account's balance is always its starting balance plus every transaction on
it. It is never stored as a number that can drift out of step with the entries
that are meant to explain it. If a balance looks wrong, a transaction is wrong,
and the entries are all there to check.

### Available and Saved

Home splits your money in two. **Available** is what there is to spend;
**Saved** is what is set aside.

The app cannot work out which is which on its own — a current account you never
touch is savings, and cash in a tin can be savings too. So each account decides
for itself. Open the account and turn on **Money set aside** for anything you
are not planning to spend, whatever kind of account it is.

By default it follows the account's type: Savings, Investment and Pension count
as set aside, everything else counts as available. Turning the switch on or off
overrides that for good; leaving it alone lets the account follow its type if
you change it later.

Nothing is counted twice, and nothing is left out: every account is in exactly
one of the two.

### The monthly rollover

At the start of each month, the app:

- posts any regular payments that are due,
- carries the closing balances over as the new opening balances,
- writes an archive record of the finished month,
- leaves every existing entry untouched.

It happens by itself. **Settings → Run the monthly update now** does it
immediately if you would rather not wait.

The archive is a permanent snapshot. If you correct a March transaction in June,
June's figures update — and March's archived figures still show what was
believed at the time. That is deliberate: history should not quietly rewrite
itself.

### Tapping the charts

The charts are not just pictures.

- **Tap a slice of the spending pie** (or its legend row) and a panel slides up
  listing every entry behind it, with the same category last month underneath so
  you can see whether this month is normal. Tap any entry to edit it.
- **Tap a column of the income-against-spending chart** and the whole dashboard
  moves to that month.
- **Tap the running-balance line** on a cash flow report to read that day's
  figure.

On the Reports screen, tapping a slice highlights it and shows its share of the
total; tapping it again clears the selection.

### Household, person or account

Chips at the top of the Home screen and the Reports screen switch between the
whole household, one person, or one account. Every figure and chart below
follows.

## Advice and what is coming

**More → Advice**, or the Advice card on the home screen.

This is the part a spreadsheet cannot do. It looks at what you have recorded and
says, in plain words:

- **Where spending has moved** — "You have spent £90 on takeaways this month —
  £30 more than your usual £60 (50% up on the last 3 months)", and what that
  would come to over a year.
- **Where it has fallen**, which is worth knowing too.
- **Where most of it goes** — your three biggest categories, their share, and
  what trimming them by a tenth would free up.
- **Your savings rate** — the share of income you keep, against the 15% usually
  suggested.
- **Whether your savings would cover an emergency**, measured in months of your
  own real bills rather than a round number.
- **What your small regular payments add up to.** Four subscriptions at a tenner
  is £480 a year.
- **What is likely to happen next** — your balance projected six months ahead,
  and a warning naming the month if it is heading below zero.

Two things it will not do:

- **It will not guess.** Each piece of advice needs enough history to mean
  something; below that it stays quiet rather than reading significance into one
  unusual month. Early on it will say so.
- **It will not tell you off.** It reports what the figures show and what the
  arithmetic implies. What to do about it is your call.

The projection assumes your regular payments carry on and that everything else
stays near its recent average. It is arithmetic, not a promise.

## Reports and printing

**Reports** offers:

- Monthly spending, yearly spending, category breakdown
- Income vs expenses, account balances, net worth
- Cash flow, savings history
- A full financial summary containing all of the above

Choose the report, the period and who it covers. Then:

- **Print** (the printer icon) — sends it to any printer your phone can see, or
  saves it as a PDF.
- **Three dots → Export** — PDF, CSV or Excel, ready to share or email.
- **Three dots → Print landscape** — for wide tables.

Reports are laid out to print cleanly on A4. Long text wraps rather than being
cut off, headings never end up stranded at the bottom of a page, and every page
carries the title and the period.

## Importing your existing spreadsheet

See [IMPORT.md](IMPORT.md) for a step-by-step walkthrough.

In short: **More → Import a spreadsheet**, then choose the file.

If your sheet has a column of figures for each person — the way the original
"Book r and h" workbook is built — the app recognises that and offers to import
the whole thing in one tap, working out who the people are, which blocks are
balances and which are bills, and which rows are totals to leave out. Otherwise
you tell it what each column means.

Either way you see exactly what it plans to create before anything is written,
so a wrong guess costs nothing.

## Backups

**Settings → Backup and restore**

- **Save a backup now** — writes a single file wherever you choose. If you have
  Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox installed, they appear in the chooser, which
  is how you get a cloud backup without the app ever holding your cloud
  password.
- **Back up automatically** — once a day, keeping the most recent few. Choose
  the folder once.
- **Restore from a backup** — the app shows you what the file contains before
  replacing anything.

A backup is a readable text file containing your whole financial history. Anyone
who can open it can read your finances, so keep it somewhere private.

**Take a backup before deleting anything large, and before reinstalling.**

## Locking the app

**Settings → Lock and security**

1. Set a PIN first — it is the fallback when a fingerprint is not recognised.
2. Then choose how to unlock: PIN, fingerprint or face, or fingerprint with a
   PIN fallback.
3. Choose how long the app can sit in the background before it locks.

Your PIN is never stored. What is stored is a scrambled version of it that
cannot practically be turned back into the PIN, held in encrypted storage
protected by the phone's own security hardware.

---

## Notifications

**Settings → Notifications**

Each can be turned on separately:

- Bills due soon (using each bill's own notice period)
- Overdue payments
- Low balance
- Savings milestones — at a quarter, half, three quarters and complete

The app checks once a day at the hour you choose. It never wakes your phone more
often than that, which is why it uses so little battery.

## Making it yours

- **Settings → Dashboard layout** — turn cards on and off and change their
  order.
- **Settings → Appearance** — light or dark, wallpaper colours, larger text.
- **More → Categories** — add, rename, recolour and group your own categories,
  with an optional monthly budget for each.

Built-in categories can be renamed and recoloured but not deleted, because the
importer and the reports fall back to them. Archive one instead — it disappears
from the lists without breaking anything that refers to it.

## Common questions

**Does it need an internet connection?**
No. Everything works offline. The only optional use of the internet is fetching
exchange rates and the bank holiday calendar, and that is off by default.

**Where is my data?**
In a database on the phone, inside the app's private storage, which other apps
cannot read. It is not sent anywhere.

**What happens if I lose my phone?**
Whatever is in your most recent backup is what you can recover. Turn automatic
backups on.

**Can two people use it on two phones?**
Not at the same time — there is no sync. You can, however, back up on one phone
and restore on the other to move everything across.

**I have thousands of transactions. Will it slow down?**
No. Searching, filtering and totalling are all done by the database rather than
in the app, and the list only draws the rows on screen.

**Can I get my data out?**
Yes, in four ways: a JSON backup, a CSV export, an Excel export, or a PDF
report. Nothing is locked in.
